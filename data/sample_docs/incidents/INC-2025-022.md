# Card payments failing after HSM firmware update

## Summary

On Monday 14 July 2025 a firmware update applied to HSM cluster B caused cryptogram checks to fail for a share of contactless and chip transactions. CardSwitch sends roughly half of its cryptogram checks to cluster B, so about one in three chip and contactless authorisations was declined for 2 hours 35 minutes. The firmware was rolled back and service returned to normal at 11:15.

This was not a certificate, configuration or capacity problem. It was a defect in the vendor firmware, which the HSM supplier has since confirmed and fixed.

## Impact

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration of customer impact | 08:40 to 11:15 (2 hours 35 minutes) |
| Declined card authorisations | 18,400 |
| Customers affected | about 11,900 |
| Value of declined transactions | about £0.9 million |
| Channels affected | Point-of-sale chip and contactless. Online card payments were not affected. |

Most customers retried with another card or paid another way. We received 640 complaints and paid goodwill credits in 37 cases where a customer was left stranded (fuel, transport).

## Root cause

Firmware 3.2 contained a defect in how it handled one cryptogram version used by some older chip cards and by most contactless transactions. The HSM returned a verification failure instead of a success, so CardSwitch declined the authorisation.

The update had been tested in pre-production, but the pre-production test pack only used a small set of modern test cards and did not include the affected cryptogram version. The change record treated the firmware update as low risk because the previous three firmware updates had gone through without issue.

**Cause category:** HSM firmware defect (not one of the recurring payment-failure causes).

## Resolution

We removed cluster B from the CardSwitch pool to stop the bleeding, then rolled back the firmware to version 3.1 and returned the cluster to service. The supplier issued firmware 3.2.1 on 4 August 2025. It was applied to both clusters on 19 August after a full test with the extended card set, with no issues.

## Lessons learned

- Splitting the update across two clusters limited the impact. Had both clusters been updated together, nearly all chip and contactless payments would have failed.
- The overall decline-rate alert took 25 minutes to fire because the rise was gradual. Per-cluster alerts would have caught it within minutes.
- We had no runbook for card authorisation failures, so the on-call engineer worked from memory. That gap was later closed by RB-007, Card Authorisation Failure Triage.
- Test data must reflect the real card estate, not just the cards that happen to be easy to obtain.
