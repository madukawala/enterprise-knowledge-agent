# Standing orders failed after scheduler configuration change

## Summary

On Thursday 5 November 2026 the Scheduler 06:00 run failed to pay about 148,000 of the 312,000 standing orders and scheduled payments due that day. A configuration change made the evening before cut the timeout for account lookups to CoreLedger from 30 seconds to 3 seconds, to speed up the run. Straight after the end-of-day batch, many lookups take longer than 3 seconds, so those payments were marked as failed.

Failed items were re-run manually from 10:15 and all were paid by 11:30, about four hours late. Under PS-005 they would otherwise have waited for the automatic retry at 14:00.

This is the third configuration incident in the last year, after INC-2026-007 and INC-2026-027. The change was not released with a canary, even though POL-005 has required one since April 2026. The Scheduler canary action was still open at the time.

## Root cause

The change was raised as a standard change, as a small performance tweak. Pre-production testing passed because CoreLedger there is always fast. In production, lookups in the first hour after the end-of-day batch are slower while caches warm up, and many take 3 to 8 seconds.

POL-005 requires a canary (5% of traffic for 30 minutes) and a rollback plan for production configuration changes. The Scheduler runs once a day as a batch, so there was no built-in way to run 5% of it first. The action to build a Scheduler canary, a 05:30 dry run on a sample of standing orders, was raised after the April policy revision but was still open in Q4 2026. In the meantime, Scheduler changes went in without one.

**Cause category:** configuration change deployed without a canary (third occurrence, after INC-2026-007 and INC-2026-027).

## Resolution

The timeout was restored to 30 seconds and failed items were re-run once checks against CoreLedger were complete. Payments Operations refunded fees caused by the late payments.

## Lessons learned

- A policy that requires a canary is not enough if some systems cannot run one. Those gaps need to be listed and closed.
- Pre-production behaves differently from production straight after the end-of-day batch. Timeouts must be set from production data.
- The same cause has now hit routing rules, fraud thresholds and the Scheduler. It is a bank-wide pattern.
