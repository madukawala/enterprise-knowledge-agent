# Month-end payment failures and read-replica change

## Summary

On Monday 31 August 2026, the last working day of the month, the PayHub database connection pool was exhausted between 10:50 and 12:05. This happened even though the pool had been raised to 350 connections per pod group after INC-2026-021. About 7,400 payment submissions failed.

Read-only work was the main driver. A new Payments Operations reconciliation dashboard and month-end reports were running heavy queries against the primary `payhub-db` database through the same pool as payment writes. At the peak, read-only queries held about 45% of connections.

This is the fourth connection pool outage this year, after INC-2026-003, INC-2026-009 and INC-2026-021, with an early warning in INC-2025-031. It led to the decision to move read-only queries to a read replica, which was planned after INC-2026-021 but not yet built.

## Root cause

PayHub uses one connection pool and one primary database for everything. Payment writes, customer status lookups, operations dashboards and reports all compete for the same connections. The new reconciliation dashboard went live on 17 August and refreshed every 30 seconds with queries on `payment_instructions`. It had been load tested on its own, but not alongside month-end payment traffic.

Raising the pool to 350 after INC-2026-021 helped but could not keep up with growing read demand. The read-replica design by Sofia Marchetti had been approved in July but the build was planned for October.

**Cause category:** PayHub database connection pool exhaustion at peak (fourth occurrence, after INC-2026-003, INC-2026-009 and INC-2026-021).

## Resolution

Payments Operations paused the dashboard and reports, freeing connections. On 1 September the Payments Platform leadership, led by Priya Raman, agreed to bring forward the read-replica change. A `payhub-db` read replica with its own pool was put live on 10 September. Customer status lookups, dashboards, reports and reconciliation now read from the replica. The primary pool is used only for payment writes.

## Lessons learned

- Four incidents with the same cause show that tuning the pool was treating the symptom. Separating reads from writes fixed the design.
- Operations tools are production load. They need the same capacity checks as customer features.
- An approved design that waits for a later quarter still leaves the risk in place.
