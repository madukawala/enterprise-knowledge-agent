# Phishing campaign led to compromised staff credentials

## Summary

On Wednesday 6 May 2026 a targeted phishing campaign sent emails to 212 staff, pretending to be a HelixID password expiry notice. Fourteen people clicked the link and three entered their username and password on the fake page. For one of those accounts, belonging to a Payments Operations analyst, the attacker sent repeated sign-in approval requests until one was approved. The attacker had access to the account for 38 minutes before it was disabled.

The account had no privileged access and the attacker did not reach any payment system or customer data. RB-008, Suspected Staff Account Compromise Response, was followed. This was not a payment failure incident.

## Root cause

The phishing email was well made and used our own branding and HelixID wording. Email filtering did not block the look-alike domain because it had been registered only two days earlier.

The attacker got past multi-factor authentication because HelixID used simple approve/deny push requests. The analyst approved one after many repeated prompts, thinking it was a system fault.

**Cause category:** phishing leading to staff credential compromise.

## Resolution

The account was disabled and all sessions revoked within 38 minutes of sign-in. The four forwarded emails contained internal process notes and no customer data. The analyst's account was restored with a new password and a new authentication device on 7 May.

## Lessons learned

- RB-008 worked well. Containment was quick once the alert was seen.
- Push approval fatigue is a known attack. Number matching should have been in place already.
- Staff who are unsure about a sign-in request should report it, not approve it. The awareness training now says this clearly.
- POL-006 kept this contained: the account had no privileged access.
