# Salary-day payment failures in PayHub

## Summary

On Friday 30 January 2026, salary day for most of our business customers, PayHub ran out of database connections at 08:05. For 2 hours 35 minutes a large share of payment submissions failed, including payroll bulk files and customer transfers from Helix Mobile and Helix Online. All payroll files were eventually processed the same day, but many employees of our business customers were paid several hours late.

The cause was the same pattern first seen in INC-2025-031 at quarter-end: a slow query on the `payment_instructions` table holding connections open, and a pool of 200 connections per pod group that was too small for the peak.

## Root cause

The duplicate-check query run for every new payment instruction scanned a large part of the `payment_instructions` table, which had grown to about 310 million rows. On salary day each query took up to 4 seconds instead of 50 milliseconds, so connections were held far longer than normal. With the pool fixed at 200 connections per pod group, new requests could not get a connection and failed.

The follow-up actions from INC-2025-031, which would have added the index and reviewed the pool size, had not been completed.

**Cause category:** PayHub database connection pool exhaustion at peak (first full outage; early warning in INC-2025-031).

## Resolution

We throttled bulk file intake and paused reports so that fewer slow queries ran at the same time. An index was considered during the incident but not added, because building it on a 310-million-row table at peak load was judged too risky. Once the payroll peak passed, the pool recovered and the backlog cleared. Payments Operations contacted the 40 largest payroll customers directly.

The index on `payment_instructions (debtor_account_id, status, created_at)` was added on 6 February 2026, out of hours. The pool was left at 200 connections per pod group while the effect of the index was measured.

## Lessons learned

- The warning signs were there in September 2025. Unfinished follow-up actions from a SEV3 turned into a SEV1.
- Restarting pods does not fix slow queries.
- An alert that does not page is easy to ignore on a busy morning.
- Salary day and month-end must be treated as known peak events, with checks and a load test beforehand.
