# Payment status stuck at pending and duplicate payments

## Summary

On Wednesday 22 April 2026 the Payment Status Service (PSS) fell far behind in reading events from the Kafka topic `payments.status`. From 11:40 to 15:10 payments made in Helix Mobile and Helix Online stayed at "pending" even though most had completed. Customers thought their payments had failed and made them again, which created about 2,870 duplicate payments.

This is the first Kafka consumer lag incident in PSS in the last year. RB-004 was written as a follow-up.

## Impact

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Duration of customer impact | 11:40 to 15:10 (3 hours 30 minutes) |
| Peak consumer lag | about 1.4 million messages |
| Customers who saw a pending status for more than 10 minutes | about 19,600 |
| Duplicate payments created | about 2,870 |
| Value of duplicate payments | about £1.12 million |
| Payment notifications delayed | up to 3 hours 20 minutes |
| Complaints | 1,640 |

The payments themselves were processed correctly by PayHub. Only the status shown to customers and the notifications were delayed.

## Timeline

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 11:20 | The notification provider starts responding slowly (2 to 4 seconds per call). |
| 11:40 | PSS consumer lag starts growing quickly. |
| 12:10 | Contact centre reports customers asking why payments are "pending". |
| 12:22 | Lag alert pages the Payments Platform on-call engineer. |
| 12:45 | Major incident declared. Status page updated using RB-009. |
| 13:05 | PSS consumers manually scaled from 8 to 24 pods, one per partition. Lag starts to fall slowly. |
| 13:30 | Notification sending switched off in PSS to speed up status updates. |
| 15:10 | Lag cleared. Statuses up to date. Notifications switched back on. |
| 16:00 | Payments Operations starts identifying duplicate payments. |
| 24 April | All duplicates found and refunded. Incident closed. |

## Root cause

PSS ran a fixed consumer group of 8 pods for a topic with 24 partitions. Each consumer updated the payment status and then sent the customer notification in the same step. When the notification provider slowed down, each message took much longer to process and the consumers fell behind. There was no autoscaling based on lag.

Helix Mobile and Helix Online let a customer submit the same payment again with nothing to stop it. PayHub had no idempotency key on payment requests, so each retry became a new payment.

**Cause category:** Kafka consumer lag in the Payment Status Service (first occurrence).

## Resolution

We scaled consumers by hand and temporarily switched off notifications. Payments Operations matched duplicates by payer, payee, amount and time, contacted customers, and refunded 2,870 payments by 24 April.

## Lessons learned

- A slow downstream service can stall the whole consumer if it sits in the same processing step.
- When customers cannot see that a payment worked, they will pay again. Duplicate protection must live in PayHub, not rely on customer behaviour.
- The lag alert threshold was too high. We lost 40 minutes before anyone was paged.
