## Discussion

### Review of INC-2026-007

Priya summarised INC-2026-007 from 17 February 2026 (SEV2). A routing rule in the Instant Payments Gateway was changed to send a group of sort codes through a new FastLane participant setting. The rule had a typo in one range. Because the change went straight to all production traffic, a share of outgoing instant payments were rejected by the scheme for most of the afternoon. Customers saw instant transfers fail and many fell back to standard transfers.

The change had been approved as a "standard configuration change", which under POL-005 only needs peer review. It was not tested against real traffic and there was no written rollback step. The engineer reverted it by hand once the cause was found, but finding the right previous value took time.

### How configuration changes are treated today

Alejandro explained that POL-005 was written in 2025 mainly with code releases in mind. Code releases already go through the pipeline with a canary stage on Kubernetes. Configuration, such as routing rules, feature flags, fraud thresholds and scheduler settings, is often changed through admin screens or a config repository that deploys to all instances at once.

Points raised:

- Jonas said that fraud thresholds in Sentinel are tuned often, sometimes weekly, and that a heavy process could slow down the response to new fraud patterns. He asked for an emergency route.
- Tomasz noted that most of the near misses his team has seen in the last year were config, not code.
- Elena said card routing tables in CardSwitch have the same exposure.
- Hannah pointed out that the bank cannot show the regulator a consistent control for configuration changes, which is a finding risk at the next audit.

### Proposal: mandatory canary release and rollback plan

Sofia presented the proposal:

1. Any configuration change that can affect payment or card processing in production must go to a canary first: a small share of traffic or a single instance, for at least 30 minutes, with named metrics to watch.
2. Every such change must have a written rollback plan, including the exact previous value and who can perform the rollback.
3. Changes made through admin screens must be logged with before and after values.
4. Emergency changes are allowed, but need approval from the on-call service owner and must still record the rollback step. They are reviewed at the next CAB.

There was broad agreement. Jonas accepted the proposal provided the emergency route is fast enough for fraud response. Priya asked that IPG routing rules be the first to use the canary process, as they caused this incident.

### Impact on teams and tooling

- The config repository pipeline needs a canary stage. Tomasz estimated three to four weeks for PayHub and IPG.
- Admin screens in Sentinel and Scheduler do not support partial rollout today. Until they do, changes there need a second person present and a timed check after the change.
- RB-005, Rolling Back a Bad Configuration Change, is in final review and will be published tomorrow. It will be referenced from the policy.

## Decisions

1. The CAB supports revising POL-005 to require canary releases and rollback plans for production configuration changes affecting payments and cards.
2. Until the policy is revised, IPG routing rule changes must go through a canary by hand, with SRE watching.
3. An emergency change route is kept, with retrospective review at CAB.
4. RB-005 becomes the standard reference for rolling back configuration.
