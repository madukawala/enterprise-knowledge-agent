## Discussion

### INC-2026-024: FastLane scheme outage

Priya summarised the incident. On 11 June FastLane was unavailable for all participants for a little over two hours, from late morning. NorthClear later confirmed a failed database switch-over in their primary data centre. For Helix this was a SEV1: no instant payments could be sent or received during the outage, and customers trying Helix Instant Transfer got delays and, later, returned payments.

Daniel said the contact centre had its busiest day of the year so far. Business customers were most affected, as several rely on instant payments for supplier runs.

### History of FastLane problems

Kwame listed the FastLane events affecting Helix over the past year:

- INC-2026-012 (16 March, SEV2): FastLane slowed down and IPG requests hit the 10-second scheme timeout. NorthClear cited a capacity problem.
- INC-2026-024 (11 June, SEV1): full scheme outage.
- Several shorter slowdowns below incident threshold, logged by Site Reliability.

After INC-2026-012, Kwame asked NorthClear for a capacity plan and a commitment on timeout rates. The capacity plan arrived late and did not include figures for peak days. No timeout commitment was given.

### How the IPG degraded mode performed

The queue-and-retry degraded mode from RB-003 was switched to automatic at the end of May, two weeks before the outage. Tomasz reported:

- IPG detected the rising timeouts and moved into degraded mode within about two minutes, with no manual action.
- Customers were told their payment was being processed, rather than seeing an error.
- The retry window is 60 minutes. Because the outage lasted more than two hours, many queued payments were returned to customers once the window passed. Some customers then retried, and when the scheme came back the queue drained in a burst.

The group agreed degraded mode did what it was designed to do, and that the failure rate would have been much worse without it. The open question is what should happen in a long outage. Options discussed: a longer retry window, offering customers a switch to a standard transfer, or both. Hannah noted any longer window must still meet the customer promise in PS-001 and the cut-off rules in POL-007.

### Contract, service levels and reporting

Kwame reminded the group that the NorthClear agreement gives availability targets but only weak duties on incident notification. During INC-2026-024 the first message from NorthClear arrived about 35 minutes after Helix's own alerts. Under POL-010, NorthClear is a critical third party, so the bank should have stronger notification terms and a right to see root-cause reports.

Sofia asked whether a secondary connection or a second scheme route is possible. There is no alternative instant payment scheme in the market, so the focus has to be on resilience at both ends.

### Joint session with NorthClear

NorthClear's representatives apologised for the outage and walked through their root cause. They committed to:

- A written root-cause report to all participants within 20 working days.
- Testing database switch-over in their primary data centre every quarter.
- Notifying participants of a scheme-wide incident within 15 minutes, through their participant portal and by phone to a named contact.

They would not yet commit to a timeout rate target for peak days, but agreed to share monthly latency figures per participant.

## Decisions

1. Treat NorthClear as a high-risk critical supplier under POL-010 until the root-cause report is received and assessed.
2. Keep IPG degraded mode as automatic. Review the retry window and long-outage handling.
3. Seek a contract change for incident notification within 15 minutes and a right to receive root-cause reports.
4. Hold the NorthClear vendor review quarterly instead of yearly.
