## Discussion

### INC-2026-019: phishing campaign and compromised staff credentials

Jonas presented the incident. On 6 May 2026 a targeted phishing campaign reached staff mailboxes, mostly in Payments Operations and the contact centre. The emails imitated a HelixID password expiry notice and linked to a look-alike sign-in page hosted on a newly registered domain. A small number of staff entered their credentials. For at least one account, the attacker also obtained a push approval for multi-factor authentication by sending repeated prompts until the user accepted one.

The compromised account had standard access, not privileged access. The attacker signed in from an unfamiliar location and browsed internal pages and a payments operations queue for roughly 50 minutes. Sentinel and the operations queue controls stopped the one attempt to change a beneficiary record, because it required a second approver. No customer funds were moved and no customer data export was found in the logs. The incident was rated SEV1 because a valid staff session was in hostile hands.

### Effectiveness of RB-008 during the response

RB-008, Suspected Staff Account Compromise Response, was used for the first time in a live incident. Overall it worked:

- The account was disabled and all HelixID sessions revoked within 20 minutes of the first report from a member of staff.
- Mail rules were checked for forwarding, and the phishing emails were pulled from all mailboxes.
- Credentials were reset for every user who clicked the link, whether or not they reported entering a password.

Gaps found:

- The runbook did not say who contacts the user's line manager out of hours. This delayed the interview with the affected member of staff.
- Revoking sessions did not end an already open session on one internal application that keeps its own session tokens. Sofia will list applications that do not honour HelixID session revocation.

### HelixID and privileged access controls

Fatima proposed moving all staff from push approval to number matching or a hardware security key, starting with Payments Operations, the contact centre and anyone with privileged access under POL-006. Tomasz confirmed that break-glass accounts for production systems already require a hardware key and were not affected.

The committee discussed impossible-travel and new-location rules in HelixID. Jonas will lower the risk threshold so that sign-ins from new countries require step-up authentication for all staff.

### Staff awareness and HR follow-up

Grace said the affected staff reported the emails quickly once they realised, and the committee agreed this should be recognised, not punished. HR will run a short targeted session for the affected teams and a bank-wide reminder about how to report suspicious emails. Grace asked that phishing simulation results are not used in performance reviews.

Hannah confirmed the incident does not meet the threshold for a personal data breach notification, based on current evidence. She will keep the assessment open until the log review is complete.

### Fraud monitoring update

Arjun reported no rise in fraud losses linked to the incident. As a precaution, Sentinel rules for new beneficiaries created by staff accounts were tightened for 30 days. He noted the planned change to card-not-present thresholds in July must follow the revised POL-005 canary process, and asked Jonas to agree the emergency route in advance.

## Decisions

1. Replace push approval with number matching for all staff by end of June, and hardware keys for privileged users by end of Q3.
2. Update RB-008 with out-of-hours contact steps and a list of applications needing separate session revocation.
3. Staff who report phishing are recognised. Simulation results are not used for performance management.
4. Keep the data breach assessment open until log review is complete.
