## Discussion

### Recap of the incident

On salary day, PayHub started failing outgoing payments shortly after the morning peak began. Payments from business customers' bulk files and retail transfers were rejected or timed out. The incident ran for most of the morning before service was stable. Customers saw "payment could not be processed" in Helix Mobile and Helix Online, and many tried again. The status page was updated, but about 40 minutes after the first alert, which Daniel said was too late given the volume of calls to the contact centre.

### Root cause

Priya presented the technical findings:

- The `payhub-db` connection pool reached its limit of 200 connections per pod group. New requests waited for a connection, then timed out.
- Connections were held for too long because of a slow query on the `payment_instructions` table. The query scans recent instructions by debtor account and has no supporting index.
- The index was agreed at the Q4 reliability review (MTG-001) and was due before salary day. The change was raised but scheduled for the following week because the change slot clashed with a CoreLedger release. Nobody flagged that the deadline mattered.
- This is the same pattern as the quarter-end slowdown in September 2025 (INC-2025-031), but at higher volume.

Sofia added that adding more pods does not help, because each pod group shares the same pool limit and the database itself has a maximum connection count. Simply raising the pool would move the bottleneck to Postgres.

### What went well and what did not

Went well:

- The new pool usage panel in Grafana showed the problem clearly once people looked at it.
- Payments Operations quickly identified which bulk files had partly failed and resubmitted them in the afternoon.

Did not go well:

- The 80 per cent pool alert fired, but it was routed to a low-priority channel. On-call was paged only when error rates rose.
- There was no runbook for pool exhaustion. Engineers restarted pods, which briefly made things worse because connections were re-established all at once.
- No load test was done before salary day, although it was an open action from INC-2025-031.

### Customer and staff impact

Daniel confirmed all failed payments were either resubmitted by the bank or retried by customers by the end of the day. Grace raised that a number of business customers' staff were paid late, and that some of the bank's own contractor payments were also delayed. Hannah will assess whether the incident needs to be reported to the regulator under the operational resilience rules, and will consider goodwill payments for customers charged late fees by third parties.

## Decisions

1. Deploy the `payment_instructions` index as an emergency change this week. Do not wait for the next change slot.
2. Keep the pool at 200 connections per pod group for now. Sofia and Priya will check database capacity before any increase.
3. Run a load test at salary-day volume before each month-end, starting with February.
4. Route the pool usage alert to the paging on-call rota.
5. Write a runbook for pool exhaustion so that the restart mistake is not repeated.
