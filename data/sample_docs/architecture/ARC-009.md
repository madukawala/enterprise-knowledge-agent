# Disaster Recovery and Data Centre Failover

## Purpose and scope

This document describes how Helix Commercial Bank keeps its critical services running if a data centre is lost, and how we move services between the primary site in Northbridge and the secondary site. It sets recovery targets, explains the failover approach and records known gaps. It is confidential because it describes recovery times and weaknesses.

The design is owned by Enterprise Architecture (Sofia Marchetti) and reviewed every year.

## Context

The bank runs two data centres about 40 km apart. The primary site, NB1, carries most workloads. The secondary site, NB2, runs some services active-active and holds standby copies of the rest. Our regulator expects important business services to stay within agreed impact tolerances, and the board has set a maximum tolerable outage of 2 hours for payments and cards.

Two events shaped this revision:

- INC-2025-041 (3 December 2025) showed that the two CardSwitch sites can drift apart in configuration, so a "healthy" secondary may not be ready to take over.
- INC-2026-003 (30 January 2026) showed that a failover would not help with load problems in `payhub-db`, because the standby has the same pool limits and the same slow queries.

## Components

| System | Pattern | RTO | RPO |
|---|---|---|---|
| Edge Gateway | Active-active, NB1 and NB2 | 5 minutes | Not applicable |
| CardSwitch and Network Gateway | Active-active, both sites take network traffic | 5 minutes | Zero (card store synchronous within site, async across sites, under 5 seconds) |
| Sentinel | Active-active | 5 minutes | Feature store rebuilt from events |
| PayHub and `payhub-db` | Active at NB1, warm standby at NB2 | 30 minutes | Under 30 seconds |
| IPG | Active at NB1, cold standby at NB2 | 45 minutes | Under 30 seconds |
| Kafka and PSS | Active at NB1, replicated cluster at NB2 | 30 minutes | Under 60 seconds |
| CoreLedger | Active at NB1, warm standby at NB2 | 60 minutes | Under 15 seconds |
| PeopleHub | Hosted at NB1, nightly backup to NB2 | 24 hours | 24 hours |

RTO is the time to restore service; RPO is the maximum data that could be lost.

## Data flow

Full site failover from NB1 to NB2, as rehearsed:

1. The major incident manager declares a site incident and the disaster recovery lead (Site Reliability) takes command.
2. Edge Gateway traffic is moved to NB2 nodes by changing the global traffic manager weights.
3. CardSwitch NB1 is drained; the card networks send all traffic to NB2.
4. CoreLedger replication is stopped and the NB2 standby is promoted. Core Banking confirms the last applied transaction.
5. `payhub-db` and the Kafka cluster are promoted at NB2. PayHub, PSS and the Scheduler are started at NB2.
6. IPG is started at NB2 and NorthClear is asked to switch our connection to the NB2 private link.
7. Payments Operations reconciles in-flight payments. The status page is updated throughout, following RB-009.

Failback follows the same steps in reverse once NB1 is stable, normally in a planned window.

## Resilience and failure modes

| Scenario | Expected handling |
|---|---|
| Loss of NB1 power or network | Full failover as above, within 60 minutes for payments |
| Loss of a single critical system at NB1 | Fail over that system only |
| Data corruption replicated to NB2 | Restore from point-in-time backup; RPO up to 4 hours |
| Configuration drift between sites | Secondary fails on takeover; detected only by test traffic |

## Security

- NB2 has the same network zones and firewall rules as NB1, described in ARC-006.
- HSMs at NB2 hold copies of all keys, loaded under dual control.
- Break-glass accounts for failover are kept in the privileged access vault and need two approvers under POL-006.

## Known limitations

- The last full failover test, in October 2025, took 2 hours 40 minutes for payments, beyond the 2-hour tolerance. IPG start-up and NorthClear link switching were the slowest steps.
- Certificates and configuration are deployed to each site separately. There is no automated check that NB1 and NB2 match.
- The PayHub standby has the same connection pool limits as the primary, so it gives no extra capacity at peak.
- PeopleHub has a 24-hour RPO. A loss near payroll submission day could delay staff pay.

Planned for 2026: IPG warm standby, a monthly drift check between sites and a half-yearly failover test.
