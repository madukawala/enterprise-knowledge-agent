# Data Classification and Handling

## Purpose

This policy defines how Helix Commercial Bank classifies information and how each class must be handled. Every document, data set and system record has one of four access levels. The access level decides who may see the information, where it may be stored and how it may be shared.

Consistent classification also lets internal tools, including the staff knowledge base, show each person only the documents they are entitled to see.

## Scope

This policy applies to all information created, received or held by the bank, in any format, and to everyone who handles it, including contractors and third parties. It supports the Information Security Policy (POL-001).

## The four access levels

| Access level | Who may see it | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Anyone. May be shared outside the bank. | Published product information, public policies such as Acceptable Use of Technology, customer-facing fee tables, status page messages. |
| **Internal** | All Helix staff and approved contractors. Not for sharing outside the bank. | Architecture overviews, most runbooks, internal product specifications, meeting notes from reliability reviews. |
| **Confidential** | Named teams only. | Customer impact data from incidents, vendor contract terms and pricing, disaster recovery plans, certificate management detail, savings interest calculation methods. |
| **Restricted** | Need-to-know only, approved individually. | Security controls and their configuration, fraud rules and score thresholds, privileged access arrangements, investigations into staff accounts. |

If in doubt, choose the higher level and ask the information owner.

## Policy statements

1. Every document must carry an access level in its front matter or header when it is created. Unlabelled documents are treated as confidential until labelled.
2. The owner team sets the access level and reviews it when the content changes.
3. Public information must be approved by the owner team and, for customer-facing material, by Marketing and Compliance before release.
4. Internal information may be shared freely inside the bank but must not be sent to personal email or posted outside the bank.
5. Confidential information may only be shared with the named teams listed by the owner. It must be stored in team spaces with access controls, not in open shared folders.
6. Restricted information must only be stored in systems that log every access. Access is granted by the owner for a stated reason and reviewed every quarter.
7. Restricted information must not be printed, copied into chat tools or included in presentations without the owner's written agreement.
8. Confidential and restricted information sent outside the bank must be encrypted and sent only to parties covered by a signed agreement under POL-010.
9. Customer personal data is at least confidential. Card numbers, PINs and authentication data are restricted and must never appear in documents, tickets or logs.
10. Information must be kept only as long as the retention schedule allows and then securely destroyed.
11. Search and knowledge tools must enforce access levels at the point of retrieval, so a user never sees content above their level.

## Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CISO (Fatima Haddad) | Owns this policy and approves changes to the access levels. |
| Fraud and Security | Provides labelling tools, monitors for leaks and grants restricted access on owner approval. |
| Information owner teams | Classify their information and keep the list of named teams up to date. |
| Operational Risk | Checks a sample of documents each quarter for correct labelling. |
| All staff | Label what they create and handle information according to its level. |

## Exceptions

Exceptions, for example a temporary need to share confidential data with a new supplier before a contract is signed, must be approved in writing by the information owner and the CISO. They are recorded in the exceptions register with an end date.
