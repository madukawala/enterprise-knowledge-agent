# Information Security Policy

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Helix Commercial Bank protects the confidentiality, integrity and availability of its information and systems. It is the top-level security policy. More detailed policies, such as Data Classification and Handling (POL-003), Incident Management and Severity Levels (POL-004) and Privileged Access Management (POL-006), sit underneath it and must be read alongside it.

Our customers trust us with their money and their personal data. A security failure can stop payments, expose customer information and damage that trust. This policy explains the minimum standard every member of staff, contractor and supplier must meet.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- all permanent and temporary staff, contractors and agency workers;
- all third parties who access bank systems or data, under the terms of the Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management policy (POL-010);
- all systems that store or process bank information, including PayHub, CardSwitch, CoreLedger, Sentinel, PeopleHub, Helix Mobile, Helix Online and the Edge Gateway;
- information in any form, whether electronic, printed or spoken.

## Policy statements

1. Every information asset must have a named owner team. The owner is accountable for its classification, access and protection.
2. All information must be classified under POL-003 and handled according to its access level.
3. Staff must sign in to bank systems with their personal HelixID account. Shared accounts are not permitted except where approved in writing by the CISO.
4. Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for HelixID, for all remote access and for all access to production systems.
5. Access must be granted on a least-privilege basis and reviewed at least every six months. Privileged access is governed by POL-006.
6. Customer data and payment data must be encrypted in transit and at rest. Connections between internal systems carrying payment traffic must use mutual TLS (mTLS) where the system supports it.
7. Cryptographic keys for PIN and cryptogram checks must be held in hardware security modules (HSMs) and must never be exported in clear text.
8. Certificates and keys must be tracked in the central certificate inventory with a named owner and an expiry date. Owners must renew them before expiry.
9. All production changes must follow the Change Management Policy (POL-005).
10. Security events must be logged centrally and kept for at least 13 months. Logs must be protected against tampering.
11. Suspected security incidents must be reported straight away to the Security Operations mailbox or through the on-call service, and handled under POL-004.
12. Staff must complete security awareness training when they join and once a year after that. Phishing simulation results are reported to line managers.
13. Systems must be patched in line with the vulnerability management standard: critical vulnerabilities within 14 days, high within 30 days.

## Exceptions

Where a team cannot meet a policy statement, it must raise an exception request with the Fraud and Security team. The request must describe the risk, the compensating controls and a date for full compliance. Exceptions are approved by the CISO for up to 12 months and recorded in the Operational Risk exceptions register. Expired exceptions are treated as policy breaches.
