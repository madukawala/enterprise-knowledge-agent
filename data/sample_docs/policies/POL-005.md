# Change Management Policy

## Purpose

This policy makes sure that changes to production systems at Helix Commercial Bank are planned, tested, approved and reversible. Many of our serious outages start with a change.

## Scope

This policy applies to every change to production, including:

- application code releases;
- infrastructure changes, such as Kubernetes, database and network changes;
- **configuration changes**, including payment routing rules, fraud thresholds in Sentinel, Scheduler settings, feature flags and connection pool settings;
- certificate and key changes;
- changes made by third parties on our behalf.

It covers all production systems, including PayHub, CardSwitch, the Instant Payments Gateway, CoreLedger, the Payment Status Service, Sentinel and Scheduler.

## Change types

| Type | Description | Approval |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Pre-approved, low-risk and repeatable, with a tested procedure. | Pre-approved by the Change Advisory Board (CAB). |
| Normal | Any change that is not standard. | Peer review plus CAB approval. High-risk changes also need the system owner's head of team. |
| Emergency | Needed to restore service or fix a serious vulnerability. | Incident lead and one senior engineer. Reviewed at the next CAB. |

## Policy statements

1. Every production change must have a change record with a description, risk assessment, test evidence, implementation plan and named implementer.
2. Changes must be peer reviewed by someone other than the author.
3. Changes must not be made during change freeze windows: the last two working days of each month, the working day before salary day, and the CoreLedger end-of-day batch window (21:00 to 05:30), unless approved as an emergency change.
4. **Configuration changes are treated in the same way as code changes.** They must be version controlled and deployed through the pipeline, not edited by hand in production.
5. **All normal changes that affect payment flows must be released by canary.** The change goes first to a small share of traffic or a single instance (normally 5% or less) for at least 30 minutes, with defined success measures, before full rollout.
6. **Every normal and emergency change must have a written rollback plan** that states how to reverse the change, how long it takes and who decides to roll back. The rollback must be tested in a non-production environment for high-risk changes.
7. Implementers must watch the agreed dashboards during and after the change. If success measures are breached, they must roll back first and investigate afterwards, using RB-005 (Rolling Back a Bad Configuration Change).
8. Changes to certificates must be applied to every site, including the secondary CardSwitch site, and checked on each site before the change is closed.
9. Changes that fail or cause an incident must be reviewed at the next CAB.

## Revision of April 2026

In February 2026, INC-2026-007 occurred when a routing rule change to the Instant Payments Gateway was deployed straight to production with no canary and no rollback plan. A large share of instant payments was rejected until the rule was reversed by hand. The Change Advisory Board reviewed this and other configuration incidents in March 2026 (MTG-003).

As a result, version 2.0 of this policy was approved by the CAB in the week of 16 April 2026. It added statements 4 to 8: configuration is treated as code, canary releases are mandatory for changes affecting payment flows, and every change needs a rollback plan. Teams had until 2026-06-30 to move hand-edited configuration into the pipeline.

## Roles and responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Core Banking engineering lead (Alejandro Ruiz) | Owns this policy and chairs the CAB. |
| Change Advisory Board | Approves normal changes, reviews failed changes and maintains the standard change list. |
| Change implementers | Follow the plan, run the canary, watch dashboards and roll back when needed. |
| System owner teams | Make sure their pipelines support canary releases and rollback. |
| Site Reliability | Provides canary tooling and dashboards. |
| Operational Risk | Audits compliance with this policy each quarter. |

## Exceptions

Where a system cannot yet support a canary release, the owner team must record an exception with the CAB, describe compensating controls (for example, a release in a quiet period with a tested rollback) and give a date for full support. Exceptions last no longer than six months.
