# Privileged Access Management

## Purpose

Privileged accounts can change production systems, read customer data in bulk or switch off security controls. If one of these accounts is misused or taken over, the damage can be severe. This policy sets the controls that apply to privileged access at Helix Commercial Bank.

This document is restricted because it describes how privileged access is controlled. Do not forward it or copy it into other documents.

## Scope

This policy applies to any account or role that can:

- administer servers, Kubernetes clusters, databases or network devices;
- change configuration in PayHub, CardSwitch, the Instant Payments Gateway, CoreLedger, Sentinel or Scheduler;
- manage HSMs, certificates or cryptographic keys;
- administer HelixID, including resetting other users' credentials;
- read or export customer data outside normal application screens.

It covers staff, contractors and third-party support engineers.

## Policy statements

1. Privileged access must be held in a separate administrator account, never in a person's everyday HelixID account.
2. Privileged accounts must use phishing-resistant multi-factor authentication with a hardware security key. Codes sent by text message are not allowed.
3. Standing privileged access to production is not permitted. Engineers request time-limited elevation through the privileged access vault. The default elevation lasts four hours and the maximum is 12 hours.
4. Every elevation request must reference a change record or incident number. Requests without one are refused automatically.
5. Elevation to the following tiers needs a second approver from the owner team:
   - Tier 0: HelixID administration, HSM administration, the privileged access vault itself;
   - Tier 1: production databases, including `payhub-db` and the CoreLedger database, and Sentinel rule configuration.
6. All privileged sessions must go through the session gateway, which records keystrokes and screen output. Recordings are kept for 13 months.
7. Shared service accounts must have passwords or keys held in the vault and rotated at least every 90 days, or immediately when someone with knowledge of them leaves.
8. **Break-glass accounts** exist for each critical system for use when the vault is unavailable. Their credentials are sealed, stored in two separate safes and may only be used during a SEV1 incident with the major incident manager's agreement. Any use is reviewed by the CISO within two working days and the credentials are then rotated.
9. Privileged access must be reviewed every quarter by the owner team. Unused entitlements over 90 days old are removed.
10. Third-party engineers may only receive privileged access for a named ticket, under supervision of a Helix engineer, and in line with POL-010.
11. Alerts must be raised for privileged logins outside approved hours, from unusual locations or without a matching ticket. Fraud and Security investigates every such alert.
12. If a privileged account is suspected of being compromised, it must be disabled at once and RB-008 (Suspected Staff Account Compromise Response) followed.

| Operational Risk | Samples privileged sessions each quarter and reports findings. |
| Privileged users | Use elevation only for the stated task and end sessions when done. |

## Changes after the May 2026 credential compromise

Following INC-2026-019, where a phishing campaign led to compromised staff credentials, the Security Steering Committee agreed to:

- require hardware security keys for all privileged accounts (statement 2), completed in July 2026;
- reduce the default elevation time from eight to four hours;
- add the "no ticket, no elevation" rule (statement 4).

## Exceptions

Exceptions can only be approved by the CISO, must be time-limited to 90 days and must be recorded in the restricted section of the exceptions register. No exceptions are permitted for Tier 0 controls.
