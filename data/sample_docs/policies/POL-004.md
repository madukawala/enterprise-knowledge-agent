# Incident Management and Severity Levels

## Purpose

This policy sets out how Helix Commercial Bank detects, classifies, manages and learns from incidents that affect customers, payments, systems or information. It defines the three severity levels used across the bank, so that everyone uses the same language and the right people are involved quickly.

## Scope

This policy covers all technology and security incidents affecting production systems, including PayHub, CardSwitch, the Instant Payments Gateway, CoreLedger, the Payment Status Service, Sentinel, Helix Mobile, Helix Online and PeopleHub. It also covers incidents caused by third parties such as NorthClear. Purely physical incidents, such as a fire alarm, follow the facilities procedure.

## Severity levels

| Severity | Definition | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SEV1** | Major, customer-facing outage. A core service is unavailable or failing for a large number of customers, or there is a serious security breach. | Card payments declined across the estate; instant payments stopped; confirmed compromise of staff credentials. | Page on-call and the major incident manager at once. Incident bridge open within 15 minutes. Status page updated within 30 minutes. Executive update every hour. |
| **SEV2** | Partial or degraded service. Some customers or some payment types are affected, or a service is slow, but there is a workaround or limited impact. | Month-end bulk payments delayed; payment status stuck at pending for some users; one CardSwitch site failing. | Page on-call. Major incident manager decides whether to open a bridge. Status page updated if customers can see the problem. Update stakeholders every two hours. |
| **SEV3** | Minor issue with little or no customer impact, or a problem caught before customers noticed. | Delayed notifications; end-of-day batch finishing late but before branches open. | Handled in working hours by the owner team. Logged and reviewed. |

The incident lead may raise or lower the severity at any time as facts emerge. When unsure, start higher.

## Policy statements

1. Anyone who suspects an incident must report it straight away through the on-call service or the Service Desk.
2. Every incident must have a single incident lead. For SEV1, this is the major incident manager (Liam O'Connor or a deputy from the rota).
3. Every incident must be recorded with an INC identifier in the format INC-YYYY-NNN.
4. Customer communication follows RB-009 (Major Incident Communication and Status Page Updates). The status page at status.helixbank.example must reflect the real state of service.
5. Incidents involving personal data or fraud must be referred to Fraud and Security and Operational Risk within one hour of being identified.
6. Regulatory notification decisions are made by Operational Risk. Reportable incidents must be notified within the regulator's time limits.
7. SEV1 and SEV2 incidents require a written incident report within five working days and a post-incident review meeting within ten working days.
8. Reports must state the root cause, the impact in numbers and follow-up actions with owners and due dates.
9. Follow-up actions are tracked to closure. Overdue actions are reported in the quarterly Payments Reliability Review and to the Operational Risk committee.
10. Where a root cause repeats, the report must link the earlier incidents and explain why the earlier fixes did not prevent it.
11. Post-incident reviews are blameless. The aim is to fix systems and processes, not to blame individuals.

## Exceptions

There are no exceptions to the reporting and recording requirements. Timescales for incident reports may be extended by the SRE lead where an investigation depends on a third party, with the reason recorded.
